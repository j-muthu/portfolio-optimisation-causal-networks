\section{Context and motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

Portfolio optimisation divides capital across assets so as to maximise return
for a given level of risk. Since Markowitz's mean--variance model
\cite{markowitz1952portfolio}, almost all of its machinery has rested on one
object: the covariance --- equivalently, the correlation --- matrix of
returns. Its most successful recent representative, Hierarchical Risk Parity
(HRP) \cite{lopezdeprado2016hrp}, sidesteps the notorious instability of
inverting a noisy covariance matrix by \emph{clustering} assets on a
correlation-derived distance and allocating by recursive bisection down the
resulting tree. Clustering is the first step of the algorithm, and what it is
fed is correlation.

Correlation brings two distinct deficits to that step. The first is a deficit
of \emph{information}: correlation is undirected --- it records that two
assets co-move, but not which one moves the other --- and it can be spurious,
because two assets responding to a common cause are statistically
indistinguishable from two assets genuinely linked \cite{simon1954spurious,
lopezdeprado2023causal}. The second is a deficit of \emph{interface}:
hierarchical clustering accepts only symmetric matrices, so even if a
directed description of the market were available, HRP and its descendants
--- including Hierarchical Sensitivity Parity (HSP) \cite{rodriguez2023hsp}
--- could not represent it. If asset $A$ drives asset $B$ but not conversely,
neither the correlation matrix nor the dendrogram built from it can say so.
These deficits are separable, and this thesis treats them separately: the
first asks whether a better \emph{input} to hierarchical allocation exists;
the second, whether an allocator can be built that \emph{consumes} what the
better input contains. They become requirements R1 and R2 of
Section~\ref{sec:question}.

Causal discovery supplies the candidate input. Score-based structure learning
--- NOTEARS \cite{zheng2018notears} and its temporal extension DYNOTEARS
\cite{pamfil2020dynotears} --- makes it tractable to fit, at every monthly
rebalance, a directed acyclic graph (DAG) over a hundred assets: an estimate
not only of which assets are linked but of \emph{which way influence flows}
between them. A small literature now applies such graphs to equity markets
\cite{howard2025causal,tang2024trading}, but it stops short of the allocation
step: no published work sets hierarchical portfolio weights from a discovered
graph, and none asks what the graph's directionality is worth once it gets
there.

That question is worth splitting, because a DAG carries two separable kinds
of information. Its \emph{skeleton} --- the undirected pattern of which asset
pairs are causally linked --- can be handed to an unmodified hierarchical
allocator by symmetrising the adjacency matrix. Its \emph{edge orientations}
--- the direction of each link --- cannot: a symmetric clustering step
discards them entirely, so exploiting them requires new allocation machinery.
Building that machinery, and using it to measure what each layer of the graph
is worth, is the methodological contribution of this thesis. Either outcome
is informative. If orientation adds nothing, the skeleton was all that
mattered and cheap symmetrisation suffices; if it adds value, every
correlation-fed hierarchical allocator has been leaving information on the
table.

The answer matters to three audiences. For the practitioner Q is a costed
question --- symmetrising a graph is free, exploiting orientation requires
the new allocation machinery of this thesis, and discovering the graph at
all costs hours of compute per backtest --- so the decomposition is a price
list: it says which layers of causal structure are worth paying for. For
the causal-finance literature, which now builds asset graphs at scale but
has not yet set portfolio weights from them, it says where in the graph
allocative value actually resides. And for research practice in a field
with a documented backtest-overfitting problem
\cite{bailey2014deflated,lopezdeprado2023causal}, the project demonstrates
that an effect of this size can be measured with a pre-registered design,
a deterministic pipeline and multiplicity-corrected inference --- and
reported honestly when it does not clear the bar.

\section{The scientific question}
\label{sec:question}

The thesis answers one precisely-stated question, returned to throughout:

\begin{quote}
\noindent\fbox{\parbox{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}{%
\textbf{Q.}\ \emph{When the correlation matrix in hierarchical portfolio
allocation is replaced by a discovered causal graph, how much of the
performance gain is attributable to the graph's \textbf{skeleton} versus its
\textbf{edge orientations}?}}}
\end{quote}

\noindent Every term is operationalised once, here, and held fixed for the
whole study:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item \emph{Hierarchical portfolio allocation}: HRP's two-stage scheme ---
    a symmetric distance matrix determines an asset ordering, and recursive
    bisection with inverse-cluster-variance splits determines the weights ---
    long-only, fully invested, on the ${\sim}100$ largest S\&P~500
    constituents (a fixed 99-name universe), rebalanced monthly over
    2007--2024 (215 rebalances at the one-year estimation window; the
    two-year window's longer burn-in leaves 203).
  \item \emph{The correlation matrix}: the graph-blind control \textsc{corr}
    is textbook HRP --- distance $\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}(1-\rho_{ij})}$ on the
    lookback window's sample correlation \cite{lopezdeprado2016hrp}.
  \item \emph{A discovered causal graph}: the contemporaneous asset--asset
    block of a DYNOTEARS fit on the joint driver--asset panel at each
    rebalance (primary), with VARLiNGAM \cite{hyvarinen2010varlingam} and a
    ridge-regularised Granger-causal graph \cite{granger1969investigating} as
    comparators; one- and two-year estimation windows (252/504 trading days).
  \item \emph{Performance gain}: out-of-sample (walk-forward), net-of-cost
    (5\,bps per unit one-way turnover, swept over $\{0,5,10,20\}$\,bps),
    annualised Sharpe ratio of daily portfolio returns --- supplemented,
    because daily returns here have excess kurtosis
    ${\approx}\,\rsKurtosis$, by the Probabilistic and Deflated Sharpe ratios
    \cite{bailey2012psr,bailey2014deflated}, and adjudicated family-wise by
    White's Reality Check, Hansen's SPA and the Model Confidence Set
    \cite{white2000reality,hansen2005spa,hansen2011mcs} over all \rsNtrials\
    configurations evaluated in the project.
  \item \emph{Skeleton versus orientations}: a \emph{fixed-graph ablation}.
    The identical per-window graph $M$ feeds allocators that differ only in
    what they read from it: D0 symmetrises $M$ before clustering (skeleton
    only), while five orientation-aware allocators (D1, D2, D2s, D3, D4;
    Chapter~\ref{chap:sensitivity}) consume the directed matrix itself.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Q presupposes two requirements, each tested in its own right:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{R\arabic*}, itemsep=1pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item \emph{The causal graph must improve on the correlation matrix at all}
    --- otherwise there is no gain to decompose. Tested like-for-like: the
    same HRP machinery fed the symmetrised graph (D0) versus the sample
    correlation (\textsc{corr}), in Section~\ref{sec:r1}.
  \item \emph{An allocator must be able to exploit orientation} --- symmetric
    clustering cannot, so the orientation-aware family of
    Chapter~\ref{chap:sensitivity} is built for the purpose: unit-verified to
    respond to edge direction, while the controls are provably
    orientation-blind (transpose-invariant), and pre-registered before any
    result was computed (Section~\ref{sec:ablation-design}).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Granted both, Q reduces to two contrasts with a shared anchor,
answered in order in Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation}: the \emph{skeleton rung},
$\text{D0}-\textsc{corr}$ --- what is it worth to know which pairs are
genuinely linked? --- and the \emph{orientation rung},
$\text{D}^{*}-\text{D0}$ for the orientation-aware variants $\text{D}^{*}$
--- holding the graph fixed, what is it worth to also know which way each
link points? The two rungs sum to the total causal-graph gain, so nothing is
left unattributed. What counts as success is therefore any precisely
estimated, honestly adjudicated answer at the two rungs --- including a
null: the failure mode the design guards against is not a small gain but an
unattributable one.

\section{Challenges and contributions}
\label{sec:challenges}

The work is organised as three technical challenges (C1--C3) and the
contributions addressing them (A1--A3), one chapter each. The mapping onto Q
is direct: C1 supplies the graph, C2 supplies the allocators that decompose
its value, and C3 supplies the inference that adjudicates the decomposition.
Together they integrate three literatures that have not previously met at
the allocation step --- causal structure learning, hierarchical portfolio
construction, and the backtest-rigour programme --- and A1--A3 are the
project's objectives: Chapters~\ref{chap:discovery}--\ref{chap:evaluation}
deliver them, and Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} assesses them jointly
against Q.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*}, itemsep=2pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item \emph{Discovering a credible asset--asset causal graph at every
    rebalance.} The graph must be estimated over a ${\sim}130$-variable joint
    panel of assets and macro drivers, under an economically-motivated
    directional prior (drivers may cause assets; single stocks do not cause
    the 10-year Treasury yield), 215 times per configuration, cheaply enough
    to sweep --- and \emph{reproducibly}: a graph that changes between runs
    invalidates every downstream comparison.
  \item \emph{Feeding a directed graph to an allocator that fundamentally
    consumes symmetric matrices.} Hierarchical clustering requires a
    symmetric distance; naive symmetrisation discards exactly the
    orientations in question. Exploiting them requires allocation machinery
    with no analogue in the HRP/HSP literature --- and an experimental design
    in which orientation is the \emph{only} treatment variable.
  \item \emph{Attributing small performance differences honestly.} The
    candidate effects are $0.01$--$0.03$ Sharpe on heavy-tailed returns,
    produced by a study that evaluated \rsNtrials\ configurations; the
    framework this project began from adds two further hazards ---
    seed-dependent neural sensitivity estimates, and a monthly feedback
    signal whose sampling noise dwarfs it.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{A\arabic*}, itemsep=2pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item \emph{A reproducible rolling causal-discovery pipeline} (addresses
    C1; Chapter~\ref{chap:discovery}): DYNOTEARS with the directional prior
    enforced natively and empirically verified to matter; a content-keyed
    discovery cache that makes the full ablation grid tractable
    (${\sim}700\times$ fan-out speedup); a data-determinism defect found and
    fixed, making every backtest bit-reproducible; and a single extraction
    chokepoint guaranteeing every allocator sees the byte-identical graph.
  \item \emph{The orientation-aware allocator family and the fixed-graph
    ablation} (addresses C2; Chapter~\ref{chap:sensitivity}) --- the core
    methodological novelty: a structural (SEM-implied) covariance that
    propagates shocks through all directed paths of the DAG;
    \emph{causal-ordered bisection}, which replaces the clustering dendrogram
    with the DAG's topological order; structural-shock risk parity; and
    co-ancestry clustering --- each deterministic, seed-free, and evaluated
    against matched skeleton-only and correlation controls on the identical
    graphs.
  \item \emph{A multiplicity-, distribution- and stability-aware evaluation}
    (addresses C3; Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation}): stationary-block-bootstrap
    contrasts for every pre-registered comparison; the
    PSR/DSR/Reality-Check/SPA/MCS battery over all \rsNtrials\ trials; a
    \rsSeedN-seed audit quantifying the neural instability of the incumbent
    HSP route; and a quantified account of why its monthly feedback loop
    cannot learn (the measurement problem).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Quantified benefits}
\label{sec:benefits}

All benefits below are measured on the protocol of
Section~\ref{sec:question} (net of 5\,bps, monthly rebalances, 2007--2024)
with significance from the Politis--Romano stationary block bootstrap
\cite{politis1994stationary}, and are reported as measured --- including
where the answer is ``little'' or ``nothing''. Full detail is in
Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation}.

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{The decomposition (the answer to Q).} At the one-year window
    the gain is skeleton-dominated: net Sharpe rises from $\rsCorrSharpe$ at
    the correlation control to $0.403$ under the skeleton alone ($+0.022$,
    pairwise $p=0.14$), and the best orientation-aware allocator adds only
    $+0.009$ more ($\rsDoneSharpe$, $p=0.47$). At the two-year window the
    split \emph{inverts}: $+0.009$ from the skeleton ($p=0.55$),
    $+0.023$--$0.027$ from orientation (pairwise $p=0.05$--$0.07$). No rung
    survives family-wise correction (Hansen's SPA:
    $p \geq \rsSpaSkelWfive$ across the four skeleton/orientation
    $\times$ window tests).
  \item \textbf{What orientation buys is consistency, not level.} The
    structural-covariance allocator D1 ($0.411/0.395$ at the two windows) and
    causal-ordered D2s ($0.406/0.399$) are the only variants strong at both;
    the skeleton-only allocator collapses from $0.403$ to $0.372$ at two
    years. The orientation edge is invariant to transaction costs up to
    20\,bps and to the graph's sparsity threshold, and D1 attains the highest
    Deflated Sharpe of all \rsNtrials\ configurations ($\rsDoneDSR$). It is,
    however, method-dependent --- absent under VARLiNGAM graphs --- and
    concentrates in calm (bottom-VIX-quintile) regimes rather than stress.
  \item \textbf{The incumbent driver route is dominated.} The HSP-style
    driver-selection pipeline is $K$-fragile; its monthly feedback loop is
    exactly inert across a $3{\times}3$ hyperparameter grid, because its
    reward has signal-to-noise ${\approx}\,\rsRewardSNR$; and its neural
    sensitivity step makes its Sharpe seed-dependent
    ($\rsSeedMin$--$\rsSeedMax$ over \rsSeedN\ seeds --- a range twice the
    $+0.010$ edge it claims over its own baseline). The headline graph-based
    allocators (D0, D1, D2s) are deterministic and exceed its best seed, and
    the 90\% Model Confidence Set over all trials excludes exactly one
    configuration: the correlation-selection baseline of the framework's
    published form.
  \item \textbf{Novelty relative to the closest published work.} Howard et
    al.\ \cite{howard2025causal} build causal graphs on the S\&P~500 but
    never set weights from them; Rodr\'iguez-Dom\'inguez
    \cite{rodriguez2023hsp} allocates hierarchically but from
    correlation-selected driver sensitivities. Setting hierarchical weights
    directly from a causal graph, decomposing skeleton from orientation on
    fixed graphs, and the orientation-aware allocator family itself are, to
    my knowledge, new.
\end{itemize}

The honest one-line summary: \emph{the causal graph's value to hierarchical
allocation lies mostly in its skeleton at short estimation windows; its
orientations add a modest consistency effect at long windows; and neither
component's contribution survives family-wise data-snooping correction.}
Establishing that decomposition, with its uncertainty stated, is the
contribution.

\section{Report structure}
\label{sec:structure}

Chapter~\ref{chap:background} analyses the three works this study builds on
--- HRP and the backtest-rigour agenda, causal graphs in equity markets, and
HSP --- and locates the gap. Chapter~\ref{chap:discovery} (A1) presents the
discovery pipeline that produces the graphs. Chapter~\ref{chap:sensitivity}
(A2) develops the allocator family and the fixed-graph ablation that make Q
answerable. Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} (A3) evaluates: the two
requirements, the decomposition itself, and how much of it survives rigorous
inference. Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion} concludes with limitations,
broader impact, and future work. Appendix~\ref{app:tables} collects the full
result tables,
operative hyperparameters and reproducibility notes; the report stands
without it.
