\chapter{A2: From Graph to Weights --- the Allocator Family}
\label{chap:sensitivity}
% ============================================================

This chapter presents the allocation machinery (A2, addressing C2) that makes
Q answerable: a family of allocators fed the identical per-window graph, split
into \emph{controls} that can see only the skeleton and \emph{treatments}
that can see the orientations. The family is the construction that discharges
requirement R2 --- before it exists, ``what is orientation worth?'' is not an
askable question. Section~\ref{sec:symm} states the problem;
Section~\ref{sec:sem} derives the structural quantities the treatments
consume; Sections~\ref{sec:controls}--\ref{sec:d-family} define the
allocators; Section~\ref{sec:ablation-design} assembles them into the
fixed-graph ablation; and Section~\ref{sec:driver-route} summarises the
driver-based route the project began from, retained as a supporting arm.

\section{The symmetric-clustering problem}
\label{sec:symm}

Hierarchical allocation (Section~\ref{sec:hrp-theme}) consumes a symmetric,
non-negative distance matrix; a causal graph is asymmetric by design, and the
asymmetry is the point. The naive fix --- symmetrise the adjacency, e.g.\
$\tfrac{1}{2}(|M| + |M^\top|)$ --- discards exactly the orientations Q asks
about. A principled-looking alternative fails too, instructively: the
bipartite double cover of the directed graph --- equivalently the Hermitian
dilation, the symmetric $2N{\times}2N$ block matrix with $M$ and $M^{\top}$
in its off-diagonal blocks --- embeds the directed matrix \emph{losslessly}
in a symmetric object, at the price of doubling the node set, so that every
asset appears once in its source role and once in its sink role.
Hierarchical allocation then produces weights over $2N$ pseudo-assets, and
recombining each asset's two role-weights into a single holding re-poses
precisely the upstream-versus-downstream question the construction was meant
to absorb: the asymmetry is relocated, not resolved. This dead end was
investigated and sets the design conclusion for the chapter --- the
symmetric interface cannot be tricked into carrying orientation; the
allocation steps themselves must change. The challenge is therefore not to
shoehorn $M$ into the existing
interface but to build allocation steps that consume the \emph{directed}
matrix natively, while changing as little else as possible, so that any
performance difference is attributable to orientation and nothing else. The
two structural observations of Section~\ref{sec:hrp-theme} open the way.
First, HRP's dendrogram exists only to produce a leaf \emph{ordering} for
recursive bisection --- and a DAG carries a natural ordering for free.
Second, HRP's bisection step consumes cluster \emph{variances} from a
covariance matrix --- and a DAG implies one.

\section{The structural model: total effects and the implied covariance}
\label{sec:sem}

Both DYNOTEARS' $W$ and VARLiNGAM's $B_0$, restricted to the asset block and
written in the $i\to j$ convention, define the structural equation model
\begin{equation}
  (I - M^\top)\, x = \varepsilon
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  x = (I - M^\top)^{-1} \varepsilon \;=:\; B\,\varepsilon,
  \label{eq:sem}
\end{equation}
where $x$ is the vector of (standardised) asset returns and $\varepsilon$ the
vector of structural shocks. Because the asset block is a DAG, its adjacency
is nilpotent, so the \emph{total-effect matrix}
$B = \sum_{k\ge 0} (M^\top)^k$ terminates in at most $N$ terms and exists
exactly --- no spectral-radius assumption. $B_{ij} = \partial x_i / \partial
\varepsilon_j$ aggregates the influence of a unit shock at asset $j$ on asset
$i$ over \emph{all} directed paths; it is the Leontief inverse of
input--output economics, and its asymmetry is where orientation enters
allocation natively. (For the non-DAG GRANGER comparator, $B$ is approximated
by the truncated series with a spectral-radius guard --- a documented
approximation.)

The SEM implies a covariance. With $\Sigma_\varepsilon =
\operatorname{diag}(\sigma^2_{\varepsilon,i})$ --- diagonality is the SEM's
own independent-shocks assumption, and estimating a dense
$\Sigma_\varepsilon$ would smuggle sample correlation back into a treatment
meant to isolate the graph --- the \emph{structural covariance} in return
units is
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma_{\mathrm{struct}}
  \;=\; D_\sigma \big( B\, \Sigma_\varepsilon\, B^\top \big) D_\sigma ,
  \label{eq:sigma-struct}
\end{equation}
where the shock variances are estimated from the fit window's structural
residuals $E = X_z (I - M)$, and $D_\sigma$ de-standardises from the
$z$-scored units the graph was fitted in back to return units. The
de-standardisation is not optional: recursive bisection compares cluster
variances, and omitting $D_\sigma$ would silently equalise asset
volatilities. Two caveats are stated rather than hidden: the asset-block SEM
marginalises over the drivers, so $\varepsilon$ absorbs driver-explained
variance and its diagonality is an approximation; and
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{struct}}$ is nearest-PSD projected and lightly ridge-loaded
so dense stress-window graphs cannot produce a singular input.

\section{The controls: correlation and skeleton}
\label{sec:controls}

\paragraph{\textsc{corr} --- the correlation matrix.} Textbook HRP
\cite{lopezdeprado2016hrp}: distance $\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}(1-\rho_{ij})}$ on the
lookback window's sample correlation, sample covariance for bisection. It is
graph-blind (verified by unit test: changing the graph cannot change its
weights) and anchors the decomposition.

\paragraph{D0 --- the skeleton.} The graph replaces the correlation matrix,
but symmetrically: each asset is embedded by its edge profile
$e_i = [\,M_{i\cdot},\, M_{\cdot i}\,]$ and the clustering distance is
$D_{ij} = \lVert e_i - e_j \rVert_2$, with sample covariance for bisection.
This embedding is \emph{provably orientation-blind}: transposing $M$
(reversing every edge) swaps the two halves of every $e_i$, leaving all
pairwise distances --- hence the dendrogram, hence the weights --- unchanged.
The property is enforced by unit test, so D0 is a true skeleton-only
allocator, not merely a weakly-directional one. D0 coincides exactly with the
asset-only Causal-HRP variant of the project's first phase (there called
V0$'$), which is what allows the new harness to be validated against a
committed historical result (Section~\ref{sec:ablation-design}).

\paragraph{D0s --- a second symmetrisation.} The same, but with the
$\tfrac{1}{2}(|M|+|M^\top|)$ distance --- included so that ``the skeleton''
is not accidentally identified with one particular symmetrisation.

\section{The orientation-aware family}
\label{sec:d-family}

Five treatments consume the directed matrix, each through a different
mechanism, and the mechanisms are chosen to \emph{separate}: D1 alters only
the bisection covariance, D2 only the ordering, D2s both, D3 abandons the
hierarchy altogether, and D4 re-enters through the clustering distance. The
results chapter can therefore localise not just whether orientation carries
value but \emph{where}. All five are long-only, fully invested, deterministic
and seed-free; all take the identical $(M,\ \text{returns window})$ inputs as
the controls; none has an analogue in the HRP/HSP literature.

\paragraph{D1 --- structural covariance.} Clustering as in D0 --- the
ordering is deliberately held at the skeleton, so that
$\text{D1}-\text{D0}$ isolates orientation's effect on the allocation step
alone --- but recursive bisection allocates on $\Sigma_{\mathrm{struct}}$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigma-struct} instead of the sample covariance: orientation
enters through the shock-propagation asymmetry of $B$.

\paragraph{D2 / D2s --- causal-ordered bisection.}
\label{sec:d2}
The clustering step is deleted entirely. HRP's dendrogram exists only to
produce a quasi-diagonalising leaf order, and a DAG supplies an order
natively: assets are sorted upstream$\to$downstream by a topological sort of
$M$ (ties broken deterministically by total downstream influence
$\sum_r |B_{ri}|$, then name), and the \emph{existing} recursive bisection
runs on that order --- with the sample covariance (D2) or with
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{struct}}$ (D2s). This is the most direct answer to the
observation that one cannot cluster on an asymmetric matrix: do not cluster.
One subtlety, discovered by the ablation's own tests, is worth recording:
recursive bisection is \emph{mirror-invariant} (reversing the leaf order
reproduces the identical partition tree), so D2 responds to orientation
through the \emph{partition structure} the order induces, not through the
upstream/downstream direction itself.

\paragraph{D3 --- structural-shock risk parity.} No hierarchy at all.
Portfolio variance under the SEM decomposes as
$w^\top \Sigma_{\mathrm{struct}}\, w =
\lVert \Sigma_\varepsilon^{1/2} B^\top D_\sigma\, w \rVert^2$, so equalising
risk contributions on $\Sigma_{\mathrm{struct}}$ is parity over
\emph{structural shock origins} --- where risk is injected into the causal
system --- rather than over correlated returns. Weights are computed by
Spinu's cyclical coordinate descent \cite{spinu2013erc} (long-only,
deterministic, convergence asserted to risk-contribution parity within
$10^{-8}$). Exact per-shock parity generally requires shorting; the long-only
solution is the practical projection, stated as such.

\paragraph{D4 --- co-ancestry clustering.} A halfway house that feeds
orientation into an \emph{unchanged} symmetric pipeline: rows of the
(row-normalised) total-effect matrix are shock-inheritance profiles, the
similarity $S = \tilde{B}\tilde{B}^\top$ makes two assets close iff they
inherit shocks from the same upstream sources --- even with no direct edge
--- and the distance $\sqrt{2(1-S_{ij})}$ enters standard HRP with sample
covariance.

\section{The fixed-graph ablation}
\label{sec:ablation-design}

The experimental design is what turns the family into an answer to Q.

\paragraph{Design.} At every rebalance the chokepoint
(Section~\ref{sec:asset-graph}) hands the \emph{byte-identical} graph $M$ to
all eight allocators
$\{\textsc{corr},\text{D0},\text{D0s},\text{D1},\text{D2},\text{D2s},
\text{D3},\text{D4}\}$; everything else --- universe, calendar, costs,
covariance-estimation window, allocator internals downstream of the distance/
covariance/ordering choice --- is shared code. Orientation is therefore the
sole treatment variable, and the decomposition is two differences with a
common anchor: $\text{D0}-\textsc{corr}$ (skeleton) and
$\text{D}^{*}-\text{D0}$ (orientation). The two rungs telescope ---
$(\text{D0}-\textsc{corr}) + (\text{D}^{*}-\text{D0}) =
\text{D}^{*}-\textsc{corr}$ --- so the total causal-graph gain is attributed
without remainder. The grid --- 7 graph-consuming allocators $\times$
\{DYNOTEARS, VARLiNGAM\} $\times$ \{252, 504\}-day windows, plus the GRANGER
arm, a sparsity sweep and a cost sweep --- was specified in a written plan
before any treatment cell was run, and no allocator was added after results
were seen.

\paragraph{Verification before results.} Three gates preceded the grid.
The \emph{unit-test gate}: 60+ tests written first, including the
orientation-blindness of the controls, the orientation-sensitivity of every
treatment, total-effect and structural-covariance identities against
hand-computed cases, ERC parity, and topological-order properties on random
DAGs. The \emph{cache gate}: all 836 (method, window) graph fits verified as
cache hits --- zero refits --- so no allocator can have seen a different
graph. The \emph{replication gate}: D0 run through the new harness reproduces
the committed first-phase V0$'$ bundle with $\Delta\text{Sharpe}=0$ and
maximum per-rebalance weight difference $0.0$ across all 215 rebalances ---
byte-perfect, establishing both that the new machinery is faithful and that
the pipeline is deterministic end-to-end.

\section{The incumbent driver route (V0, V1, V2)}
\label{sec:driver-route}

The project began from HSP's driver route, and it is retained as a supporting
arm --- both because it supplies the seed-audit and measurement-problem
evidence of Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation}, and because its failure is what
motivated Q. Its mechanics, compactly: at each rebalance $K{=}17$ drivers are
selected from the pool --- by cumulative correlation with the assets (V0, the
HSP baseline) or by the causal scores of Section~\ref{sec:selection} (V1) ---
and a per-asset feed-forward network maps the selected drivers to asset
returns; the Jacobian sensitivity vectors $s_i$ embed assets in driver space,
and HRP runs on $\lVert s_i - s_j\rVert_2$ with sample covariance. V2 closes
a feedback loop: the realised holding-period reward, the excess Sharpe over
equal-weight,
\begin{equation}
  R[t] = \mathrm{Sharpe}\big(\text{portfolio over } [t, t{+}21\text{d}]\big)
       - \mathrm{Sharpe}\big(\tfrac{1}{N}\big),
\end{equation}
is attributed to drivers in proportion to $\sum_i |w_i s_{i,d}|$, smoothed by
an EMA $U_d[t] = \gamma\,\mathrm{credit}_d[t] + (1-\gamma)U_d[t-1]$, and
blended into the next selection score
$\alpha\, z(\mathrm{causal}_d) + (1-\alpha)\, z(U_d)$, with lookahead
discipline enforced by keying utility on holding-period-\emph{end} dates and
a tested leak canary. Note the update frequency: the loop learns from one
21-observation Sharpe estimate per month --- the root of the measurement
problem analysed in Section~\ref{sec:r1}. Unlike the D-family, this route is
\emph{not} deterministic: the network initialisation seed changes the
sensitivities, hence the weights --- quantified by the 20-seed audit of
Section~\ref{sec:seed-audit}.
