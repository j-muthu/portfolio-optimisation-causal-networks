\begin{abstract}
\noindent Hierarchical portfolio allocators cluster assets on a symmetric
matrix --- in practice the return correlation matrix, which records that two
assets move together but not which moves the other. Temporal causal discovery
produces a richer input: a directed acyclic graph over the assets. A DAG
carries two separable kinds of information, its \emph{skeleton} (which pairs
are linked) and its \emph{edge orientations} (which way influence flows), and
a symmetric clustering step can use only the first. This thesis asks a single
question: \emph{when the correlation matrix in hierarchical portfolio
allocation is replaced by a discovered causal graph, how much of the
performance gain is attributable to the graph's skeleton versus its edge
orientations?} The question presupposes two requirements the thesis must
establish: \textbf{R1}, that the causal graph improves on the correlation
matrix at all; and \textbf{R2}, that an allocator can exploit orientation in
the first place. The allocator family built for R2 is the methodological
contribution.

The decomposition is made measurable by a fixed-graph ablation. At each of 215
monthly rebalances over 2007--2024, one DYNOTEARS graph is discovered over the
${\sim}100$ largest S\&P~500 constituents (VARLiNGAM and ridge-Granger graphs
serve as comparators), and that same graph feeds allocators differing only in
what they read from it: a graph-blind correlation-distance HRP control, a
skeleton allocator that symmetrises the graph before clustering, and five
orientation-aware allocators. These exploit the DAG's total-effect matrix
$B = (I - M^{\top})^{-1}$, exact by nilpotency, to build a structural
covariance for the allocation step, a topological order that replaces the
clustering dendrogram, an equal-risk allocation across structural shock
origins, and a clustering distance from shared causal ancestry. Every variant
is deterministic, seed-free and bit-reproducible. Performance means
out-of-sample, net-of-cost annualised Sharpe at one- and two-year estimation
windows, adjudicated by a multiplicity-aware battery --- probabilistic and
deflated Sharpe, White's Reality Check, Hansen's SPA, the model confidence
set --- over all \rsNtrials\ configurations evaluated.

The answer is window-dependent, and modest. At the one-year window the gain is
essentially all skeleton: net Sharpe rises from $\rsCorrSharpe$ at the control
to $0.403$ for the skeleton ($+0.022$, pairwise $p=0.14$), and orientation
adds only $+0.009$ more ($\rsDoneSharpe$, $p=0.47$). At the two-year window
the split inverts: the skeleton's contribution collapses to $+0.009$
($p=0.55$) while orientation restores $+0.023$--$0.027$ ($p=0.05$--$0.07$).
Orientation therefore buys cross-window \emph{consistency} rather than level:
the structural-covariance and causal-ordered allocators are the only variants
strong at both windows, are robust to transaction costs and graph sparsity,
and the former attains the highest deflated Sharpe ($\rsDoneDSR$) of all
\rsNtrials\ configurations. No rung of the decomposition, however, survives
family-wise correction (Hansen's SPA: skeleton
$p=\rsSpaSkelWtwo$/$\rsSpaSkelWfive$, orientation
$p=\rsSpaDirWtwo$/$\rsSpaDirWfive$ at the two windows); the effects are absent
under VARLiNGAM graphs; and the orientation edge concentrates in calm rather
than stressed regimes.

Two supporting results sharpen the verdict. The driver-selection route this
project began from, Hierarchical Sensitivity Parity, is shown to be
unlearnable at monthly frequency --- its feedback reward has a signal-to-noise
ratio of ${\approx}\,\rsRewardSNR$ --- and unstable: its neural sensitivity
step's Sharpe spans $\rsSeedMin$--$\rsSeedMax$ across \rsSeedN\ random seeds,
a range twice the edge it claims, while the graph-only allocators are
deterministic and the headline variants exceed every seed. The contribution is
thus a pre-registered, reproducible decomposition of where a causal graph's
value lies for hierarchical allocation: mostly in its skeleton at short
windows, in its orientations only as a consistency effect at long windows, and
nowhere by enough to clear a family-wise bar.
\end{abstract}

\chapter*{Declarations}

\section*{Statement of originality}
This report and the work it describes are my own. The research was conducted
under the supervision of Dr.\ Ce Guo and Prof.\ Wayne Luk. All external
sources --- published work, datasets and third-party code --- are cited in
the text; vendored third-party libraries are identified as such in the
repository. All numerical results are reproducible from the committed code
and cached data (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}).

\section*{Ethical considerations}
No Imperial ethics approval was required: the project uses only publicly
available market and macroeconomic data and involves no human participants or
personal data. The work is a retrospective backtest study; it makes no
investment recommendations, and Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation} quantifies
precisely the uncertainty with which its results should be read.

\section*{Use of generative AI}
Generative AI (Anthropic's Claude) was used in two capacities, both reviewed
by me as author. \emph{As a coding assistant} during implementation: all
AI-assisted code was human-reviewed and is covered by the project's test
suite and by the end-to-end verification gates of
Section~\ref{sec:ablation-design} --- including a byte-perfect replication
gate against an independently produced historical result --- so no
correctness claim rests on unverified generated code. \emph{As a drafting and
proofreading aid} for the prose of this report: AI-drafted text was checked
and edited line by line, and, because unverified numerical claims are a known
failure mode of generated text, every statistic quoted in this report is
either a macro generated directly from the committed results
(\texttt{\_generated/robust\_stats.tex}) or traceable to a committed results
file, as documented in Appendix~\ref{app:tables}. The research question,
design decisions, mathematics, experiments, analysis and conclusions are my
own.
