\begin{abstract}
\noindent Hierarchical portfolio allocators cluster assets on a symmetric
matrix, usually the return correlation matrix. Correlation records
which assets move together, but not which asset's movement comes first.
Causal discovery algorithms, by producing a directed graph,
recover both: a skeleton denoting which pairs are linked, 
and edge orientations indicating which way influence flows. 
A small literature discovers such graphs in equity markets, but
no published work sets hierarchical portfolio weights from a discovered
graph, so what either aspect of  the graph contributes to a portfolio is
unknown. This thesis asks: when the correlation matrix in hierarchical
portfolio allocation is replaced by a causal graph, is there a
performance improvement, and how much of any improvement is attributable
to the graph's skeleton versus its edge orientations?

I answer this by providing an identical graph to allocators 
that differ only in the amount of information they extract from the graph.
This isolates the performance gain attributable 
to the differing information extracted.
I compare an HRP baseline to two groups of graph-based allocators: 
skeleton-only and orientation-aware. 
The two successive differences sum to the total gain over the control. I
estimate this decomposition over 2007--2024, using four estimation horizons 
(nine months to two years), on a 99-asset subset of the S\&P~500. 
DYNOTEARS is the primary causal discovery algorithm used, 
with VARLiNGAM and ridge-Granger as comparators.
I measure performance with out-of-sample, net-of-cost annualised Sharpe.

The gain is small and stable in sign: the 40 graph-versus-control cells
span $-0.025$ to $+0.036$ net Sharpe (median $+0.017$; 36 positive).
Sign-stability is not significance: Hansen's SPA test rejects nowhere
over the full allocator grid ($p \geq \rsSpaSkelWfivePre$), only
marginally at the two longest horizons over a narrower family fixed
after the results were seen ($p=\rsSpaSkelWthree$, $\rsSpaSkelWfive$),
and nowhere once a pooled test prices the search across strategies and
estimation windows jointly. The composition shifts with horizon: the
skeleton carries most of the gain near the one-year convention, while
orientation's increment is positive from one year out and largest at the
longer horizons. Direct controls identify
the mechanism: a de-factored covariance with no graph reproduces the
orientation allocators' covariance-channel gain at every window, their
Deflated Sharpe ratios near-tied at the top of the study, so that gain
works through market-factor removal, not directional content; a
graph-free skeleton of matched density fails the same test, so the
skeleton's one-year gain does need the discovered graph. An
out-of-sample slice on 2025--26 data, which post-dates
every design choice, returns the same sign on every
contrast its protocol specified in advance; at 19 months, signs are
all it can certify.

The answer is therefore conditional and modest: replacing correlation
with a DYNOTEARS graph pays, and most of what it pays is available from
the skeleton alone near the one-year convention. Orientation buys
robustness to the estimation horizon, but what it adds is reproducible
by de-factoring alone, and its statistical support is marginal,
family-dependent, and absent under joint multiplicity pricing. The
neural driver route this project began from is dominated on every axis,
its monthly reward being ${\sim}95\%$ noise and its Sharpe moving with
the network seed, which is why the graph rather than the network is the
object of study.
\end{abstract}
