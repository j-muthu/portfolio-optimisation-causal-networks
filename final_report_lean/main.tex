\begin{abstract}
\noindent Hierarchical portfolio allocators cluster assets on a
\emph{symmetric} matrix --- almost always the return correlation matrix, which
is undirected, and blind to which asset moves which. Temporal causal discovery
produces a richer object: a directed acyclic graph over the assets. But a DAG
carries two separable kinds of information --- its \emph{skeleton} (which pairs
are linked) and its \emph{edge orientations} (which way influence flows) ---
and symmetric clustering keeps only the first. This dissertation asks one
question: \emph{when the correlation matrix in hierarchical portfolio
allocation is replaced by a discovered causal graph, how much of the
performance gain is attributable to the graph's skeleton versus its edge
orientations?}

The decomposition is made measurable by a \emph{fixed-graph ablation}: the same
per-window DYNOTEARS (and, as comparators, VARLiNGAM and ridge-Granger) graph
over the ${\sim}100$ largest S\&P~500 constituents feeds a family of allocators
that differ only in what they read from it --- a graph-blind
correlation-distance HRP control; a skeleton-only allocator that symmetrises
the graph before clustering; and five orientation-aware allocators built for
this study, which propagate shocks through the DAG's total-effect
(Leontief-inverse) matrix into a structural covariance, replace the clustering
dendrogram with the DAG's own topological order, allocate equal risk across
structural shock origins, or cluster on shared causal ancestry. All are
deterministic and seed-free; every backtest is bit-reproducible; 215 monthly
rebalances over 2007--2024, net of costs, at one- and two-year estimation
windows.

The answer is window-dependent, and modest. At the one-year window the causal
graph adds $+0.031$ net annualised Sharpe over the correlation control
($0.411$ vs $\rsCorrSharpe$), of which the skeleton contributes $+0.022$
($p=0.14$) and orientation only $+0.009$ ($p=0.47$). At the two-year window
the split inverts: the skeleton's contribution collapses to $+0.009$ while
orientation restores $+0.023$--$0.027$ (pairwise $p=0.05$--$0.07$) --- the
orientation-aware allocators are the only variants strong at \emph{both}
windows, and the structural-covariance allocator attains the highest Deflated
Sharpe ratio of all \rsNtrials\ configurations evaluated. Yet no single step of
the decomposition survives family-wise data-snooping correction (Hansen's SPA:
skeleton-vs-correlation $p=\rsSpaSkelWtwo$/$\rsSpaSkelWfive$;
orientation-vs-skeleton $p=\rsSpaDirWtwo$/$\rsSpaDirWfive$ at the one-/two-year
windows), the effects are absent under VARLiNGAM graphs, and the orientation
edge concentrates in calm rather than stressed regimes. Two supporting results
sharpen the picture: the driver-selection route of the framework this project
began from (HSP) is shown to be unlearnable at monthly frequency --- its
feedback reward has a signal-to-noise ratio of ${\approx}\,\rsRewardSNR$ and
its neural sensitivity step's seed variability ($\rsSeedMin$--$\rsSeedMax$
across \rsSeedN\ seeds) exceeds its own claimed edge --- and the graph-based
allocators dominate that route under every seed. The contribution is a
precise, pre-registered, reproducible decomposition of where a causal graph's
value lies for hierarchical allocation: mostly in its skeleton at short
windows, in its orientations only as a robustness effect at long windows, and
nowhere by enough to clear a family-wise bar.
\end{abstract}
