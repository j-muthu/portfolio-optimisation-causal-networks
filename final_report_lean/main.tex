\begin{abstract}
\noindent Portfolio optimisation has long relied on the correlations between
asset returns. Correlation, however, is undirected, static, and can be spurious:
a variable that merely co-moves with equities is indistinguishable from one that
causally drives them. This dissertation asks whether replacing the
correlation-based driver selection in a recent allocation framework,
Hierarchical Sensitivity Parity (HSP), with \emph{causal} structure produces
more robust portfolios. I propose \emph{closed-loop Causal-HSP}: drivers are
chosen by temporal causal discovery (DYNOTEARS, with VARLiNGAM as a comparator)
under a directional prior, assets are clustered in the resulting causal
sensitivity space, and a feedback loop lets realised performance update a
driver-utility score that shapes the next period's selection.

I evaluate the method on an approximate S\&P~100 (top-99 by market
capitalisation), 215 monthly rebalances over 2007--2024, net of costs, at
one- and two-year lookback windows, with significance from a Politis--Romano
stationary block bootstrap. The findings are deliberately characterised rather
than overstated. \textbf{(i)} Causal driver selection beats correlation
selection at the one-year window (Sharpe $0.381$ vs $0.371$ for DYNOTEARS;
$0.399$, $p=0.004$, for VARLiNGAM) but the edge vanishes at the two-year window
and is not robust to the number of drivers $K$. \textbf{(ii)} The strongest
one-year variant is \emph{asset-only} Causal-HRP (no exogenous drivers), at
Sharpe $0.403$, significantly beating both correlation-HSP ($p<0.001$) and the
driver-based method ($p=0.01$). \textbf{(iii)} The closed loop is \emph{inert}:
across an entire $\alpha/\gamma$ grid the feedback never alters the
causally-selected driver set, so it reproduces the open loop exactly. Two of
these conclusions corrected an earlier, more optimistic reading after a
data-determinism bug was found and fixed, making the whole pipeline
bit-reproducible. The contribution is a complete, reproducible characterisation
of where causal structure helps portfolio construction (the short window, and
most via asset-only structure) and where it does not (the long window, and via
the feedback extension).
\end{abstract}
