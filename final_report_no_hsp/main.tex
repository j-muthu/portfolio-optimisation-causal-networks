\begin{abstract}
\noindent Hierarchical portfolio allocators typically use 
the return correlation matrix as an input to their allocation. 
However, this lacks any notion of causality, and is therefore
vulnerable to spurious correlation.
Causal discovery algorithms ameliorate this, producing
a directed graph which can be decomposed into a skeleton
(which assets are linked) and edge orientations
(which assets causally affect others).
This thesis addresses the following question: when the
correlation matrix in hierarchical portfolio allocation is
replaced by a causal graph, is there a performance
improvement, and how much of any improvement is attributable
to the graph's skeleton versus its edge orientations?

I answer this question by providing an identical graph to
two groups of allocators: one that only takes in the graph's
skeleton, and one that takes in the whole graph, including its
edge orientations. This decomposes the performance gain into
the two aspects of the graph. I then compare these allocators
to HRP as a baseline. I estimate this decomposition over 
2007--2024, using four estimation windows 
(nine months to two years), on a 99-asset subset of the S\&P~500. 
DYNOTEARS is the primary causal discovery algorithm used, 
with VARLiNGAM and ridge-Granger as comparators.

The gain is small and stable in sign: the 40 graph-versus-control cells
span $-0.025$ to $+0.036$ net Sharpe (median $+0.017$; 36 positive).
Sign-stability is not significance: Hansen's SPA test rejects nowhere
over the full allocator grid ($p \geq \rsSpaSkelWfivePre$), only
marginally at the two longest estimation windows over a narrower family fixed
after the results were seen ($p=\rsSpaSkelWthree$, $\rsSpaSkelWfive$),
and nowhere once a pooled test prices the search across strategies and
estimation windows jointly. The composition shifts with the estimation window: the
skeleton carries most of the gain near the one-year convention, while
orientation's increment is positive from one year out and largest at the
longer estimation windows. Direct controls identify
the mechanism: a de-factored covariance with no graph reproduces the
orientation allocators' covariance-channel gain at every window, their
Deflated Sharpe ratios near-tied at the top of the study, so that gain
works through market-factor removal, not directional content; a
graph-free skeleton of matched density fails the same test, so the
skeleton's one-year gain does need the discovered graph. An
out-of-sample slice on 2025--26 data, which post-dates
every design choice, returns the same sign on every
contrast its protocol specified in advance; at 19 months, signs are
all it can certify.

The answer is therefore conditional and modest: replacing correlation
with a DYNOTEARS graph pays, and most of what it pays is available from
the skeleton alone near the one-year convention. Orientation buys
robustness to the estimation window, but what it adds is reproducible
by de-factoring alone, and its statistical support is marginal,
family-dependent, and absent under joint multiplicity pricing.
\end{abstract}
