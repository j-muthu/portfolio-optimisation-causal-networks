# structure & questions to address, from my supervising professor -- KEEP THIS STRUCTURE IN MIND
1. Think about the 3 key questions that your project should cover:
  - Q1: what are the key technical challenges the project would address? Label them C1, C2 etc. Avoid having more than three challenges unless there is a good reason.
  - Q2: what are the novel aspects of your proposed approach in addressing such challenges? Show which novel aspects address challenge C1, which other novel aspects address C2 etc.
  - Q3: what are the quantified benefits of the proposed approach, in comparison to existing published results?

2. It is never too early to think about your project report. A common organisation for the report is to have 6 chapters: introduction, background and related work, three achievements (the last could cover evaluation), and conclusion. To make the structure simple, chapter 3-5 could cover the three achievements A1..A3, such that A1 would address challenge C1, A2 would address c2 etc.

3. It is probably simplest to divide the remaining time into 3 stages:
 - Stage 1: from now to 15 June
 - Stage 2: from 16 June to 31 July
 - Stage 3: from 1 August to 9 September

You could then plan what work for A1-A3 could fit into Stage 1-3. The aim is to have a good draft of your report by 1 September.

So please think about the above, and let us know your thoughts about Q1-Q3, C1-C3, A1-A3 and your plan for Stage 1-3.

# more details on interim report
The Interim Report (formally known as the Background Report) should describe the research you have completed during the early stages of the project and your plans for the remainder of the project.

There is no mark associated with this report, but it a required component. You must complete this to an acceptable standard to pass the MSc Project overall.

It is there so that you can get some early feedback on your project (and your writing). It is also helpful for you, as much of what you write for this document can be reused as the background chapters for the Final Report. The report also provides the basis for you to have a discussion with your second marker.

Report Contents
Background Section
You should summarise the essential material related to the subject of the project, for example a review of relevant academic literature. This material can be incorporated into the project final report.

Referencing should use the Vancouver format.

Progress Section
You should also include a separate section on progress that describes: the activities and accomplishments of the project to date; any problems or obstacles that might have cropped up and how those problems or obstacles are being dealt with; and plans for the next phases of the project.

Section Headings
There are no mandated section headings for this report. The best format will vary from project to project.

A format that works well for many types of project is:

Title Page

Table of Contents

Chapter 1 - Introduction
Introduce the project and the problem. Provide some context for a reader who is not an expert in this area.

Chapter 2 - Literature Review
Describe the work that has already been completed that is relevant to the problem you are addressing.
Some projects may need an additional section covering notation, key theories or other such technical information.

Chapter 3 - Technical Achievements/Experimentation
If you have completed any early experiments or tested out different methods, you can briefly discuss them in this chapter.
This chapter will not be relevant for all projects (and students on MSc Computing/MSc AI are unlikely to have reached this stage yet)

Chapter 4 - Project Plan (incorporating ethics review, if appropriate)
Discuss the plan for the remainder of the project. Include a timeline.
It is useful to define what success in your project will look like. You might be able to compare your system against existing systems.
If your ethical review has identified that you will need to get Imperial ethical approval for your project (e.g. you are conducting research using medical data), please flag this here.

References
Use Vancouver Format

Appendices
Unlikely to be needed for this short document, but you can include supplementary information if necessary.

Note, there is no need to include an Abstract or an Acknowledgements page within this document.

Some reports may use different chapter titles. That is fine.

Report Format and Length
Please see the information about the Final Report for formatting details. It is recommend that you use the same format for the Interim Report.

The typical length for the Interim Report is 10 to 20 content pages, plus title pages, appendices, reference list etc. You should not need to exceed 20 pages.

# additional interim report suggestions from my supervisor
1. The interim does not need to be a document that includes all technical details and results. It is fine if not every method or experiment is finished. What matters most is a clear account of what you have done so far, and a realistic plan for the remaining work up to early September.

2. Please be explicit about three things: the challenge your project addresses, what is novel in your approach compared with previous work, and the quantified benefit over existing methods. It helps to state these sharply, for example as short lists, rather than only describing what you have done. This is the most common thing I find missing.

3. Show your results, and show them visually where you can. If you already have results, bring them into the report rather than only mentioning them. A figure, such as a key plot or a comparison against a baseline, often communicates far more than a table or a paragraph.

4. Please do not leave the evaluation or results chapter empty. Include whatever results you have so far, together with your evaluation plan: the metrics, the comparisons, and what you still intend to run. Use metrics that fit your project.

5. A clear structure helps. A common and effective shape is an introduction, a background chapter, two or three technical chapters, and a short conclusion/summary. It is usually better to draft one chapter fully, even as bullet points, before moving on to the next.

6. A short abstract, even a rough half page, is worth including early. Front matter such as acknowledgements can wait until the final report.
